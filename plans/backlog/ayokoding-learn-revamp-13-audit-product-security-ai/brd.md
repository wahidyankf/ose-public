# Business Requirements — Audit of Product, Security, and AI Courses

## Problem

AyoKoding (`apps/ayokoding-www`) has 45 courses on application development, AI engineering, product and leadership,
interview preparation, and security that readers can open today. They are not outlines. Most sit in the three
software-engineer learning paths, 19 of them relate to the AI Engineer path, and six are named by the capstones that
plan 08 wrote. A reader who follows a path meets them as real steps, but they do not meet the series definition of
done:

- **Most are short of their floor.** 27 of the 45 are below the word floor of their mode, by 476,972 words in all.
  `linux-app-development` has 78 example headings over 5,071 words, `agent-context-and-memory` has 48 over 1,700, and
  `build-your-own-web-framework` has 80 over 6,652. 39 courses have a drilling page under 5,000 words (130,763
  words short in all), and two capstones have no drilling page and no `learning/` folder at all.
- **No example is proven to run.** None of the 45 courses has a `run.yaml`, so the code harness (plan 05) checks
  nothing in them. They hold 2,328 code files, 1,753 example folders, and 58 kata folders, but
  2,831 units are needed: 1,007 must be created and 1,824 converted. Three mobile and desktop courses hold
  code that imports Android, Apple, or Windows frameworks that a Linux container cannot run.
- **Lessons and files disagree.** 326 anchors do not match the file they point at, and 755 code
  fences and 1,064 `Output` blocks are not tied to a file at all. A reader who copies a lesson's code may
  get a program that differs from the one the course says it ran.
- **The "Why It Matters" blocks are thin.** 1,562 are present across the courses and 733 of them are under 50 words.
- **AI facts age, and AI examples may call things that do not exist offline.** Fourteen courses teach products, model
  families, protocol revisions, prices, and limits that change within months. An example that calls a hosted model needs
  a key and a network, so it can be neither recorded nor re-run by a reader without an account.
- **Security examples can mislead or harm.** Four security courses with code, the agent-sandboxing course, and one
  capstone show attacks, probes, detections, and permission bypasses in code. Examples that open sockets, resolve
  names, run a shell, or name real addresses cannot be allowed to run in CI or to teach a reader to aim at a real
  system.
- **Six courses are templated filler.** Plan 09's filler guard flags `agent-permissions-and-sandboxing`,
  `android-app-development`, `build-your-own-reactive-ui`, `information-architecture-and-seo`, `linux-app-development`,
  and `windows-app-development`, and tags them for this plan to fix.
- **Two interview courses carry the wrong mode.** `behavioral-and-leadership-interviews` and `system-design-interview`
  are recorded as Annotated Concept but have no code folder, no code fence, and no program.

## Who This Is For

- **Readers:** software engineers, AI engineers, security learners, and interview candidates who expect each course to
  be finished: accurate text, examples they can run, drilling to test themselves, and current facts. Many arrive through
  a software-engineer path or the AI Engineer path.
- **Authors and maintainers:** people who edit a course after merge. They need a test that stops a finished course from
  becoming thin again, a harness that tells them when an example stops running, and guards that keep AI examples
  offline and security examples safe.
- **Security-minded reviewers:** people who must be able to say that no course teaches a reader to attack a real system
  and that no CI job touches one.
- **Later plans:** plan 14 re-measures harness coverage and the empty filler baseline on the finished series, and
  inherits this plan's share of both.
- **The PR reviewer:** one person reviews a very large PR and needs a commit per course and a record of each course's
  gates.
