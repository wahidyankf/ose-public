# Refused Reductions

These are out of scope permanently, not merely deprioritized:

- **Rewriting prose to save words.** Propagation's semantic-preservation hard gate forbids
  generalizing, weakening, compressing away, or paraphrasing a material qualifier. Grooming never
  paraphrases, tightens, or densifies a rule's wording. The scaffolding class deletes sentences that
  carry no obligation; it never edits one that does, and the two are not adjacent operations.
- **Weakening a qualifier.** Propagation's semantic-preservation hard gate binds every hand-off:
  audience qualifiers, scope boundaries, exceptions, pass conditions, and violation conditions
  survive verbatim enough to remain unambiguous.
- **Trimming a safety guardrail.** Secrets and `.env` rules, the Git Identity Guardrail, and
  environment-branch rules are never candidates in any class.
- **Deleting a named document's entry point.** No class removes or hollows a `<name>.md` that names
  a workflow, principle, convention, or development practice — `rules-grooming.md`,
  `root-cause-orientation.md`, `automation-over-manual.md` — without explicit per-item authorization
  naming that document. These are what the corpus links to and what agents route by; losing one
  breaks every citation of it. A `<name>/` shard folder is ordinary corpus: shards there are
  reabsorbed, deduplicated, or retired under their classes' normal rules.
- **Raising a word budget.** Never, for any reason, in any class.
- **Deleting a rule to make room.** Forbidden anti-fix 1. Retirement removes a rule that no longer
  binds; it is never a capacity measure.
